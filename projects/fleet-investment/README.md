# Fleet investment committee

Synthetic independent portfolio case by Quincy Jones, with AI-assisted construction and review. All recommendations, owner roles and monitoring observations are simulated; no employer outcomes are claimed.

## Decision

Prefer replacement in the base case, subject to comparable vendor quotes, financing/liquidity review and tax validation. Reopen the decision under the fuel-shock case, where leasing has the lowest modeled present-value cost.

Six replacements need $660,000 net upfront cash after old-vehicle sale proceeds, within the illustrative $700,000 capital limit. Base replacement has $373,698 incremental NPV versus keeping the old fleet. Its advantage over leasing is only $15,208 of PV cost, so small quote or residual changes may reverse the decision.

## Alternatives and conditions

Replacement consumes upfront cash and bears residual-value risk. Leasing preserves liquidity but adds contractual payments, usage limits and vendor dependency. Keeping the fleet avoids purchase cash but exposes higher modeled maintenance and downtime.

Confirm identical service capacity, included lease maintenance, vehicle availability, actual residual quotes and cash budget. Evaluate after-tax effects and lease accounting separately. Native sensitivity tests the real model discount rate and old-fleet maintenance input.

## Method and limitations

Five-year, pre-tax incremental cash-flow comparison. Year 0 includes net purchase/disposal cash or lease deposit/disposal proceeds. Operating cash costs cover maintenance, fuel and downtime with 3% annual inflation. Terminal receipts and deposit return occur at the end of year five. NPV uses end-year discounting.

Fuel-shock case changes fuel price, maintenance and discount rate together; it is not a fuel-only attribution. Downtime cost is assumed cash exposure, not independently observed loss. No depreciation tax shields, financing, lease-accounting entries, mileage penalties, insurance or transition costs are included.
