# Executive Decision Brief: Transportation Lane Profitability

## Decision

Which transportation lanes warrant a closer review of pricing, cost-to-serve, or service recovery?

## Recommendation

Start with the lowest-margin modeled lane. Validate its labor, fuel, accessorial, empty-mile, and overhead assumptions, then assess eligible quotes for a targeted pricing test. Track contribution margin, award conversion, and on-time performance together before expanding any change.

## Evidence from the synthetic case

- 1,200 simulated shipments across six lanes
- $958,707 modeled revenue and $710,290 modeled direct cost
- 25.9% weighted contribution margin
- 91.6% modeled on-time rate
- Detroit–Cleveland is the lowest-margin lane at 17.6%, just below the illustrative 18% review threshold
- Holding cost and shipment mix constant, the model estimates about a 0.5% revenue increase would meet that threshold

## Management actions

1. Validate the lane cost definitions and shipment mix against current source data.
2. Separate contract renewals, new bids, and customer-specific exceptions.
3. Set guardrails for award conversion and service performance alongside the margin target.
4. Review actual results with Finance and Operations before expanding a rate change.

## Risks and limitations

All records and amounts are synthetic. The scenario holds costs and shipment mix constant; it does not model demand elasticity, contract terms, competitive bids, network effects, or customer retention. The 0.5% result is a hypothesis for review, not a promised price change or realized savings.
